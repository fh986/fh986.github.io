Context: Digital reading platforms serve diverse users, yet reading ability varies widely. This project identifies key visual factors driving those differences, supporting adaptive UX design for more efficient text presentation.

Goal: Develop a data-driven framework to measure and predict reading speed, guiding text design that maximizes comprehension and efficiency.

Key Deliverables:
Created a quantitative metric that explains the largest variance in reading speed, offering actionable insights for UX Engineering to personalize reading experiences.
Research methodology and findings were presented to peer researchers at NYU Miniconvention, 2025.

Scale & Methodology: Designed and conducted a large-scale remote quantitative study (N>400) measuring psychophysical responses (visual acuity and susceptibility to clutter, or crowding). Used advanced regression and statistical modeling to rigorously validate the predictive utility of the reading performance metric.

Project Leadership: Served as lead researcher, ensuring timely execution. Aligned project strategy with the PhD advisor and other researchers during weekly meetings.

Experimental Design: Strategically calibrated the online experiment to prioritize UX efficiency (streamlined interface, minimized duration) and data reliability (rigorously controlled variables for high empirical fidelity).
