<h2 style="margin-top: 2.5rem; margin-bottom: 1rem;">The Challenge</h2>

**Predicting digital reading speed reliably.**

People vary widely in how quickly they can read, but we do not fully understand why. Digital reading platforms could better support different users if we could identify the visual factors behind these differences.
The challenge was to find reliable measures of visual performance that predict reading speed. The results support text design that maximizes **comprehension** and **efficiency**.
.

<h2 style="margin-top: 2.5rem; margin-bottom: 1rem;">The Approach</h2>

**Large-scale online survey of visual performance and reading.**

I designed a large-scale study to measure how differences in visual perception relate to differences in reading speed.
I focused on two fundamental limits of vision: **visual acuity** and susceptibility to visual clutter, or **visual crowding**. I then developed quantitative models to test how well these measures predict reading performance.

<h2 style="margin-top: 2.5rem; margin-bottom: 1rem;">The Execution</h2>

I led a remote study with more than **400** participants. I strategically calibrated the online experiment to prioritize **UX efficiency** (streamlined interface, minimized duration) and **data reliability** (rigorously controlled variables for high empirical fidelity).


I built online psychophysical tasks to measure visual acuity, crowding, and reading performance. I used regression and statistical modeling to quantify their relationships and evaluate the reliability of the resulting predictions.


<h2 style="margin-top: 2.5rem; margin-bottom: 1rem;">The Impact</h2>

The project identified **visual crowding as a key factor associated with differences in reading speed** and developed a quantitative framework for measuring this relationship.
The findings provide a foundation for predicting how visual constraints affect reading and for designing text experiences around measurable differences between users. These results offer actionable insights for UX Engineering to personalize reading experiences, supporting **adaptive UX design for more efficient text presentation**.
